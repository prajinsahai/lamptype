About LampType

A typing test that stays out of your way.

LampType is a typing speed test. You type the words you see, and it tells you how fast and how accurately you did it. There is no account, no sign-up and nothing to install.

Behind the words is a lava lamp. It drifts on its own, and it answers your typing — the blobs lift and swell as you go, and the whole field warms up the faster you type. It is meant to be noticed out of the corner of your eye and never to pull you off the line you are typing.

The three modes

ModeWhat it does
Time Type for a fixed 15, 30, 60 or 120 seconds. The passage refills, so you can never run out of words.
Words Type a fixed 10, 25, 50 or 100 words. It ends the moment you finish the last one — no trailing space needed.
1 word A timed run showing one word at a time. Finish the word and the next one appears on its own; there is no space to press and no way to read ahead. Every word is a cold start.

Any mode can add punctuation and numbers. There is also a race mode, where you type against an AI opponent that calibrates to your recent speed: random for your first five races, then matched to your average and ramping gently from there.

How the numbers are worked out

These are the standard definitions, the same ones most typing tests use, so your score here is comparable to your score elsewhere.

  • WPM — correctly typed characters divided by five, then divided by the minutes elapsed. A “word” is five characters by convention, and the space that closes a correctly typed word counts as one of them.
  • Raw — the same sum over every character you typed, right or wrong. Raw is always at or above your WPM.
  • Accuracy — a running tally of keystrokes, not a comparison of the final text. A mistake you go back and fix still costs you accuracy, because you still made it.
  • Consistency — how even your pace was, from the per-second raw speed. A flat run scores near 100; a run of bursts and pauses scores lower.

Runs shorter than one second are shown but never recorded, because a handful of characters divided by a sliver of a second is arithmetic noise rather than a score.

Typing alone never leaves your browser

Every keystroke is processed on your own machine. Typing on your own — which is every mode, including the race against the AI opponent — sends nothing anywhere, not to us and not to anyone. Your results are kept in your browser's local storage so your history and personal bests survive a reload, and you can erase all of it at any time from the history panel.

Racing a friend is the exception, because a race needs both of you in the same room. While you are in one, the site sends the name you chose, how far through the passage you are, and your speed and accuracy at the finish. The room is held in memory for as long as somebody is in it and discarded when the last person leaves; the words you actually typed are never sent, and nothing is written to a database. No room, no socket.

The one thing that does reach a third party is advertising. The privacy policy sets out exactly what that involves.

Accessibility

The test is built to be driven entirely from the keyboard, and the interface aims at WCAG 2.1 AA. Typed text is well clear of the AA contrast threshold in every theme, including over the brightest part of the lava lamp. If you set prefers-reduced-motion in your operating system, the lamp holds a single still frame and the petals stop falling.

Because tab restarts the test, use shift+tab to move focus out of the typing area. If something here does not work with your assistive technology, please tell us — it will be treated as a bug.
